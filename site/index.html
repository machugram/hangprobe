When to use it

Three moments on the desk.

Not a fleet tool. Reach for Hangprobe when the screen is frozen in front of you.

Nothing is linked into the app.

sample already ships with macOS. Hangprobe shells out to it. The process under test does not gain a framework.

One report, three places.

A Mac app, a user app in the booted Simulator, and an Android package adb can see. The fields do not change.

A hung probe exits 1.

A script can gate on hangprobe probe. The threshold stays 250 ms, which is Apple's floor for a hang.

The report

Busy, blocked, or waiting.

The heaviest main-thread leaf is the note you paste into the bug. System frames stay. The app frame is the one to read first.

kind, main_gap_ms, and the stack.

busy is on the CPU. blocked is a lock or a sleep off the run loop. responsive is the event loop. gap_at_least means the thread never returned.

FAQ

What it will not do.

Does it link a framework into the app?

No. On a Mac or the Simulator it runs /usr/bin/sample. On Android it reads a live dump over adb. Nothing is added to the binary under test.

Can it see a physical iPhone?

No. That process is not on this Mac. On-device Hang Detection and MetricKit cover the phone. Hangprobe does not grow a device-side agent to chase it.

Why is the threshold 250 ms?

That is Apple's floor for a hang. Android's input timeout is about 5 seconds. A live Android dump that is stuck is not an ANR, and an ANR trace is labeled as one.

Does sampling pause the app?

Yes. A one second probe pauses the process for about a second, longer while symbols are written. hangprobe worst samples for 10 seconds. Watch stops after the first hang so it does not sample forever.

Where does the report go?

It stays on this Mac. History is under Application Support, for the before and after. Nothing is uploaded.

Is it open source?

Yes. The whole product is MIT — desk studio, CLI, MCP, inbox, gate, and device watchdogs. No license keys.
